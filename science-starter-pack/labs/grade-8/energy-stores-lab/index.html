Energy Stores Lab

Understanding: Energy can be transferred between energy stores. Scientists track energy in eight kinds of stores. Energy moves from one store to another along four pathways, and the total never changes.

Learn

Five short lessons in order. First see why an energy store is a model. Then meet the eight stores and the four pathways, link store changes to work, and follow energy through real Alberta chains.

Lesson 1 · A way of keeping track
EN1.K1

An energy store is a model

Energy is a quantity measured in joules (J). It is not a substance you can see or hold. So how do scientists keep track of it? They use a model called energy stores.

An energy store is a label for where the energy in a system is, and how much is there. A moving toboggan has energy in its kinetic store. The same toboggan at the top of a hill has energy in its gravitational store.

A store is not a real box or tank inside an object. It is a bookkeeping tool. We work out how much is in each store from things we can measure, such as speed, height, stretch or temperature.

Adding up all the stores tells you how much energy the system has. As things happen, energy shifts from store to store, but the total stays the same unless energy enters or leaves the system.

Remember: an energy store is a model for tracking how much energy a system has and where it is. The stores change, but the total is conserved.

Lesson 2 · Eight stores
EN1.K3EN1.S1EN1.S2

The eight types of energy store

Each store is linked to something you can observe or measure:

  • Thermal: how hot something is and how much material there is.
  • Kinetic: how fast something moves and its mass.
  • Elastic: how much something is stretched, squashed, bent or twisted.
  • Gravitational: how high something is lifted above the ground.
  • Chemical: fuels, food and batteries, which can react, often with oxygen.
  • Magnetic: magnets held apart while they attract, or pushed together while they repel.
  • Electrostatic: electric charges held apart while they attract, or pushed together while they repel.
  • Nuclear: the nuclei, or centres, of atoms, as in the Sun or uranium.

Light, sound and electricity are not stores. They are ways energy travels between stores. You will meet them in Lesson 3.

Lesson 3 · Four pathways
EN1.K4EN1.S3

Energy moves between stores along pathways

Energy cannot jump from one store to another by magic. It is transferred along a pathway. There are four:

  • Mechanical work: a force moves an object, such as a push, a pull or a twist.
  • Electrical: an electric current does work as it flows through a circuit.
  • Heating: energy moves from a hotter object to a colder one, by touch (conduction) or by moving fluids (convection).
  • Radiation: energy carried by waves, such as light and infrared. Sound waves are often counted here too.

To describe a transfer, name three things: the store that empties, the pathway, and the store that fills.

Sentence frame: Energy is transferred from the ______ store of ______, by ______, to the ______ store of ______.

Lesson 4 · Store changes and work
EN1.K2

A change in a store tells you how much work was done

When a force moves an object, it does work. The work done is the amount of energy transferred:

work = force × distance
W = F × d  (N × m = J)

That energy has to end up in a store. When a hoist lifts a hay bale at a steady speed, the work done on the bale equals the increase in its gravitational store. So if you can measure the change in a store, you know how much work was done.

increase in gravitational store = weight × height
ΔEg = m × g × h  (g ≈ 9.8 N/kg on Earth)

Work is one pathway, so this link works when energy is transferred by a force. A store can also change by heating or radiation. A pot of water warming on a stove gains thermal energy without any work being done on it.

You do not need to memorize these formulas. They show how the numbers fit together.

Model: hoist a load

Lesson 5 · Follow the energy
EN1.S3EN1.K4EN1.K3

Energy chains in Alberta

Most real events link several transfers into a chain. Water held behind a dam on the Bow River has energy in its gravitational store. As it falls, it turns a turbine. A generator sends energy along wires as an electrical pathway, and a kettle in a kitchen warms up.

At every step, some energy spreads into the thermal store of the surroundings, mostly through friction and heating. The energy is not destroyed. It becomes spread out and hard to use again. This is why no machine passes on all the energy it receives as useful energy.

Big idea: the total energy is the same at the end of a chain as at the start. It is just in different stores.

Teacher notes

  • EN1.K1: the draft says a store represents how much energy is “available” within a system. The lab teaches that stores track how much energy a system has and where it is. Not all of it is usable: energy spread into the thermal store of the surroundings is still there but hard to use again (Lesson 5). Stores are presented as a bookkeeping model, not physical containers.
  • EN1.K2: the draft says a change in stores “can indicate the amount of work done”. The lab teaches this for transfers by a force: when a hoist lifts a load at steady speed, work done equals the increase in the gravitational store. It also states that heating and radiation change stores without work, so K2 does not contradict K4. W = F × d is carried from Science 7 F2; ΔEg = mgh and Ek = ½mv² appear only as context with worked examples (g = 9.8 N/kg).
  • EN1.K3: the chemical store is described as belonging to fuel or food together with oxygen where a reaction needs it. The nuclear store is shown in the Sun (fusion) and uranium (fission), named only. Magnetic and electrostatic stores are taught as attracting things held apart or repelling things pushed together.
  • EN1.K4: the four pathways follow the draft (heating, radiation, mechanical work, electrical). Heating is taught as conduction and convection, and radiation as waves such as light and infrared, with sound often grouped with radiation in this model. Science 7 EN2 listed radiation as a heating process; the lab notes that this model lists it as its own pathway. Light, sound and electricity are taught as pathways, not stores.
  • Numbers: toboggan values, hoist loads, ramp data (friction losses of about 3%, 12% and 30%) and all practice problems are made up for practice and labelled. Alberta examples (Bow River hydro dams, southern Alberta wind farms, the Edmonton LRT, a furnace, a campfire, archery) are general everyday contexts only. The statements do not name First Nations, Métis, Inuit or Francophone contributions, so none are added.
  • Not taught: calculation of thermal, elastic, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic or nuclear store amounts, and efficiency as a percentage (mentioned only as “not all useful”). Static electricity is left for EN2 and waves for EN3.
