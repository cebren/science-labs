Launch: Observation, Inference and Data

SL1Id7

Data Detective Lab

Critical thinking supports valid interpretation of data.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (7 knowledge, 6 skills)

Outcome: Students relate interpretation to scientific explanation.

Knowledge

  • SL1.K1Scientific explanations are based on observations and on interpretations of data.
  • SL1.K2Observation uses senses or tools to gather information.
  • SL1.K3Inference is an explanation based on observations and prior knowledge.
  • SL1.K4Both qualitative and quantitative data can support scientific explanations.
  • SL1.K5Critical-thinking skills involved in data interpretation include
    • categorizing and recognizing patterns
    • predicting outcomes
    • analyzing relationships, e.g., cause and effect
    • supporting or contradicting hypotheses
    • justifying conclusions
  • SL1.K6Biases can influence the collection and interpretation of data.
  • SL1.K7Science-related careers apply interpretation and scientific explanation in areas such as
    • mixtures and solutions, e.g., pharmacist
    • structures and simple machines, e.g., structural engineer
    • energy and heating, e.g., HVAC technician
    • ecosystems, e.g., conservation biologist

Skills and procedures

  • SL1.S1Differentiate between observation and inference.
  • SL1.S2Interpret qualitative and quantitative data.
  • SL1.S3Discuss how predictions and hypotheses contribute to scientific explanations.
  • SL1.S4Develop a scientific explanation based on interpretation of data.
  • SL1.S5Discuss how bias can affect scientific explanations.
  • SL1.S6Explore how a science-related career applies interpretation and scientific explanation.

Life Science: Ecosystems

LS1Bs7

Biodiversity and Stability Lab

The stability of ecosystems is influenced by biodiversity.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (10 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students analyze stability and biodiversity of ecosystems.

Knowledge

  • LS1.K1Ecosystems are dynamic and their characteristics can vary over time.
  • LS1.K2Components of ecosystems are interconnected with the following relationships:
    • species, which are the same type of organism
    • populations, which are interacting groups of the same species
    • communities, which are interacting populations
    • ecosystems, which are communities interacting with abiotic factors
  • LS1.K3Biodiversity refers to the variation
    • within organisms of the same species (genetic diversity)
    • in the number and abundance of different species (species diversity)
    • in the number of unique habitat types in an ecosystem (ecosystem diversity)
  • LS1.K4Ecosystem stability is the capacity of an ecosystem to maintain its structure, biodiversity, and processes over time.
  • LS1.K5Each species in an ecosystem occupies an ecological niche, or role in their habitat, which allows different species to co-exist in the same environment.
  • LS1.K6Factors that influence a species’ ecological niche include
    • biotic and abiotic factors
    • adaptations
    • competition
    • predation
    • symbiotic relationships
  • LS1.K7Adaptations are features that make a species well suited to a specific environment.
  • LS1.K8When species occupy distinct ecological niches, competition for resources is reduced.
  • LS1.K9Predation plays an important role in maintaining ecosystem stability.
  • LS1.K10Symbiotic relationships between different species include mutualism, commensalism, and parasitism.

Skills and procedures

  • LS1.S1Represent the interconnections between components within an ecosystem.
  • LS1.S2Investigate the biodiversity of species in various ecosystems.
  • LS1.S3Relate species adaptations to the environment in which species live.
  • LS1.S4Analyze the ecological niches of different species living in the same ecosystem.
  • LS1.S5Discuss how biodiversity supports ecosystem stability.
LS2Ef7

Energy Flow and Cycles Lab

Ecosystem stability is maintained through the processes of cycling of matter and transfer of energy.

Open the labSlide deck (opens in a new tab)
Curriculum statements (6 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students analyze stability and biodiversity of ecosystems.

Knowledge

  • LS2.K1The continuous cycling of water through ecosystems supports life.
  • LS2.K2The continuous cycling of carbon includes
    • photosynthesis
    • respiration
    • decomposition
    • emission
    • ocean exchange
    • geological processes
  • LS2.K3The Sun is the primary source of energy in most ecosystems, and this energy is harnessed by producers.
  • LS2.K4Consumers can be classified according to trophic interactions as
    • primary consumers
    • secondary consumers
    • tertiary consumers
    • apex predators
  • LS2.K5Ecosystems have more producers than consumers because each time an organism is consumed, most of the energy from that organism is eventually transferred to the environment.
  • LS2.K6Food webs and energy pyramids can be used to represent the transfer of energy through trophic interactions among producers, consumers, and decomposers.

Skills and procedures

  • LS2.S1Discuss ways in which the water cycle supports life.
  • LS2.S2Represent how matter cycles within an ecosystem.
  • LS2.S3Represent energy transfer in an ecosystem, using an energy pyramid.
  • LS2.S4Analyze transfer of energy in trophic interactions.
  • LS2.S5Predict the effects of population change, including an extinction of species, on a food web.
LS3Ds7

Disturbance and Succession Lab

Ecosystems can maintain stability despite disturbances.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (6 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students analyze stability and biodiversity of ecosystems.

Knowledge

  • LS3.K1Ecological succession is the predictable pattern of change in the types of species and habitat of an area when new areas become available for occupation (primary succession) or when existing areas undergo change (secondary succession).
  • LS3.K2Disturbances that can impact ecosystem stability include
    • geological processes
    • extreme weather events
    • human activity
  • LS3.K3Biodiversity is declining on a global scale at an increasing rate due to habitat destruction, pollution, invasive species, and climate change.
  • LS3.K4Biodiversity can be reduced when species become endangered, extirpated, or extinct.
  • LS3.K5Caring for ecosystems is a shared responsibility that can involve collaboration, monitoring data, and interventions such as
    • species relocation
    • habitat conservation
    • protecting water resources
  • LS3.K6Forest management supports ecosystem stability by using practices such as
    • selective harvesting
    • reduced impact logging
    • reforestation
    • controlled burning
    • wildfire risk reduction

Skills and procedures

  • LS3.S1Explore how ecosystem stability can be disturbed.
  • LS3.S2Discuss factors that contribute to declining biodiversity in an ecosystem.
  • LS3.S3Describe ecological succession in ecosystems.
  • LS3.S4Discuss possible ways to care for an ecosystem.
  • LS3.S5Evaluate data collected from an ecosystem with decreasing biodiversity.

Matter: Pure Substances and Mixtures

M1Cs7

Matter Sorter Lab

Matter can be classified based on physical properties and separated according to its particles.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (8 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students analyze pure substances and mixtures.

Knowledge

  • M1.K1Matter can be classified as a pure substance or mixture.
  • M1.K2A pure substance is composed of only one type of particle, and those particles cannot be separated by using physical techniques.
  • M1.K3Pure substances can be identified or described by physical properties, including
    • appearance, e.g., colour and lustre
    • density
    • freezing point
    • melting point
    • boiling point
  • M1.K4Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance d = m/V
  • M1.K5A mixture is composed of two or more types of particles, and those particles can be separated by using physical techniques.
  • M1.K6Mixtures can be classified as
    • heterogeneous, including mechanical mixtures
    • homogeneous, including solutions
  • M1.K7Techniques used to physically separate heterogeneous mixtures include
    • mechanical separation, which is based on particle size and density, e.g., sorting, sieving, and filtration
    • magnetic separation, which is based on magnetism
  • M1.K8Distillation is a technique used to physically separate homogeneous mixtures based on boiling points.

Skills and procedures

  • M1.S1Investigate physical properties of various pure substances.
  • M1.S2Represent the particle composition of pure substances and mixtures.
  • M1.S3Solve problems involving calculations related to density when given mass and volume.
  • M1.S4Classify pure substances and mixtures.
  • M1.S5Separate mixtures according to physical properties.
M2So7

Solution Mixer Lab

Particles can interact to form solutions.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (6 knowledge, 3 skills)

Outcome: Students analyze pure substances and mixtures.

Knowledge

  • M2.K1A solution is a homogeneous mixture in which solute particles dissolve in a solvent.
  • M2.K2Dissolving is the process by which particles of solute become evenly distributed among particles of a solvent.
  • M2.K3Stirring or increasing the solute’s surface area can increase the rate of dissolving.
  • M2.K4Solutions can be described by their concentration, including dilute or saturated.
  • M2.K5Solubility is a physical property that describes the amount of solute that can be dissolved in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.
  • M2.K6The amount of solute that dissolves in a specific amount of solvent can increase as the solvent’s temperature increases.

Skills and procedures

  • M2.S1Relate solubility, concentration, and dissolving.
  • M2.S2Represent various concentrations of solutions.
  • M2.S3Investigate factors that influence solubility.

Energy: Heat and Temperature

EN1Et7

Energy Accounts Lab

Transfer of energy can be used to explain change in objects and systems.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (4 knowledge, 2 skills)

Outcome: Students investigate the relationships between energy, temperature, and heating.

Knowledge

  • EN1.K1In science, energy is a quantity, not a physical substance, that is measured in joules, which indicates the amount of work that can be done on an object or in a system.
  • EN1.K2A system can be described in terms of its components and their interactions, while the surroundings are everything outside of the system.
  • EN1.K3The transfer of energy drives change, and changes in an object or system indicate that work has been done.
  • EN1.K4The total change in a system’s energy is equal to the total amount of energy that transfers into or out of the system.

Skills and procedures

  • EN1.S1Illustrate the transfer of energy into, within, and out of a system.
  • EN1.S2Analyze changes in energy when work is done.
EN2Th7

Temperature and Heating Lab

Objects or systems experience temperature changes because of energy transfer.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (9 knowledge, 4 skills)

Outcome: Students investigate the relationships between energy, temperature, and heating.

Knowledge

  • EN2.K1Temperature is a measure of the average energy of motion of the particles in an object or a system and can be used to describe hotness or coldness.
  • EN2.K2The faster the particles in an object or system move, the greater their average energy of motion and the higher the temperature.
  • EN2.K3Heating is the transfer of energy that occurs from a higher-temperature object or system to a lower-temperature object or system.
  • EN2.K4Heating occurs through the processes of
    • conduction
    • convection
    • radiation
  • EN2.K5Conduction, convection, and radiation can be used in technologies, such as
    • electric stoves, which use conduction
    • hot-air balloons, which use convection
    • microwave ovens, which use radiation
  • EN2.K6Insulation is used to slow rates of heat transfer and can be found in systems such as
    • nature, e.g., whale blubber
    • technologies, e.g., cooler
  • EN2.K7Some of the radiation from the Sun and from space is reflected or absorbed by Earth’s atmosphere and surface.
  • EN2.K8Most energy absorbed by Earth’s surface eventually radiates back up, contributing to further absorption and reflection in the atmosphere.
  • EN2.K9The absorption and reflection of radiation by Earth’s atmosphere and surface affect the temperature of Earth.

Skills and procedures

  • EN2.S1Explain how heating occurs through conduction, convection, and radiation.
  • EN2.S2Discuss how the processes of conduction, convection, or radiation are used in various technologies.
  • EN2.S3Investigate systems that minimize energy transfer.
  • EN2.S4Describe how Earth’s temperature is affected by radiation from the Sun.

Forces: Structures and Simple Machines

F1Sf7

Structures and Loads Lab

Structures can be designed and constructed to withstand different types of forces.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (9 knowledge, 6 skills)

Outcome: Students investigate the interactions of forces applied to structures and simple machines.

Knowledge

  • F1.K1Structures are composed of interacting components, such as beams, columns, and arches.
  • F1.K2Natural structures can inspire the design and construction of human-made structures.
  • F1.K3Loads are forces that act on structures and can be classified as
    • permanent, e.g., weight of the structure
    • non-permanent, e.g., wind, weight of snow, weight of occupants, and ground shifting
  • F1.K4Structural deformation from internal forces, external forces, or both can cause changes to a structure’s shape or integrity.
  • F1.K5Structural fatigue is progressive damage from repeated or cyclic loading that can lead to a structure’s failure over time.
  • F1.K6Structural deformation and failure can result from
    • flawed design
    • poor construction
    • improper load
    • structural fatigue
  • F1.K7Factors that can affect the design and construction of structures include
    • function
    • available resources
    • safety
    • intended lifespan and sustainability
  • F1.K8Natural and constructed structures can be categorized as
    • mass or solid
    • frame
    • shell
  • F1.K9Structures from different categories can be combined to better withstand a load.

Skills and procedures

  • F1.S1Develop a structure that supports a load.
  • F1.S2Identify the components and categories of a structure.
  • F1.S3Classify loads as permanent or non-permanent.
  • F1.S4Compare natural structures to human-made structures.
  • F1.S5Investigate a structure failure.
  • F1.S6Compare the effectiveness of structures that have a common function but different designs.
F2Sm7

Machines and Work Lab

Machines can change the force required to complete a task.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (7 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students investigate the interactions of forces applied to structures and simple machines.

Knowledge

  • F2.K1Force, expressed in newtons, has both magnitude and direction.
  • F2.K2Work, expressed in joules, is done when an applied force moves an object a distance in the direction of the force W =F ×d.
  • F2.K3Simple machines can accomplish a task by changing the magnitude or direction of the input force and include
    • inclined planes
    • levers
    • wheels and axles
    • screws
    • wedges
    • pulleys
  • F2.K4In a simple machine, the input work is equal to the output work.
  • F2.K5Mechanical advantage occurs when a machine reduces the input force needed to move an object by increasing the distance over which the force is applied.
  • F2.K6Complex machines, such as a wheelbarrow or a can opener, are made by combining simple machines.
  • F2.K7A complex machine’s mechanical advantage is the product of the mechanical advantages of the simple machines.

Skills and procedures

  • F2.S1Analyze work done in a local setting involving simple machines.
  • F2.S2Explain the relationship between work, force, and distance.
  • F2.S3Solve problems involving calculations related to work when given force and distance.
  • F2.S4Explain why the amount of work does not change when a simple machine is used.
  • F2.S5Compare the mechanical advantage of various simple and complex machines.
CS3Dp7

Design Process Studio Lab

Design processes can be used to solve problems.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (6 knowledge, 3 skills)

Outcome: Students examine how design processes can be used to arrive at solutions.

Computer Science

CS1Pi7

Programs and Instructions Lab

Computers use programs to perform tasks.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (9 knowledge, 7 skills)

Outcome: Students explore ways to communicate with computer systems.

Knowledge

  • CS1.K1A program is a set of instructions written in a programming language that a computer can run to perform tasks.
  • CS1.K2Decomposition is the process of breaking a task into smaller parts, which can be accomplished by using functions.
  • CS1.K3Functions are organized blocks of code designed to perform specific tasks within a program.
  • CS1.K4Most programming languages include built-in functions for common tasks, such as
    • draw
    • add
    • print
    • move
  • CS1.K5A function has a descriptive name, inputs, an algorithm, and an output and can be used within
    • sequences
    • loops
    • conditionals, e.g., if–then–else
  • CS1.K6The input–process–output, or IPO, cycle is a model describing how computer systems use hardware and software to input data, process it, and generate output.
  • CS1.K7Software consists of code that instructs hardware, the physical components of a computer.
  • CS1.K8Types of software include
    • system software, e.g., operating systems that enable users to efficiently operate a computer
    • programming software, e.g., tools that help programmers code
    • application software, e.g., word processing or data processing programs
  • CS1.K9An interface is a device or program that can enable communication between
    • software and hardware, e.g., printer driver
    • user and computer, e.g., touch screen

Skills and procedures

  • CS1.S1Decompose a larger task into smaller tasks.
  • CS1.S2Explain a built-in function within a sequence of visual block-based code.
  • CS1.S3Create a sequence using various built-in functions within visual block-based code.
  • CS1.S4Compare two or more sequences with different functions that achieve the same result.
  • CS1.S5Discuss potential improvements to existing code.
  • CS1.S6Model the IPO cycle.
  • CS1.S7Explore various configurations of hardware and software.

Space

SP1Te7

Moon and Tides Lab

Interactions of celestial bodies are observed and experienced on Earth.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (5 knowledge, 3 skills)

Outcome: Students investigate natural phenomena in relation to interactions of celestial bodies.

Knowledge

  • SP1.K1Natural phenomena that result from interactions of celestial bodies include tides, eclipses, and lunar phases.
  • SP1.K2Tides result from gravitational forces of the Sun and the Moon.
  • SP1.K3A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun, casting a shadow on a small portion of Earth, totally or partially blocking the view of the Sun from that area.

    • new moon
    • first quarter moon
    • full moon
    • last quarter moon

  • SP1.S2Explain lunar and solar eclipses in relation to the position of Earth, the Moon, and the Sun.

Earth Science

ES1We7

Weathering and Erosion Lab

Earth’s surface is constantly changing due to the dynamic interactions of various geological processes.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (10 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students examine how geological processes continually reshape Earth’s surface.

Knowledge

  • ES1.K1Earth is a dynamic planet that is shaped by internal and external geological processes.
  • ES1.K2Hydrological processes, such as wave action, glacial processes, and groundwater flow, contribute to geological processes.
  • ES1.K3The geological processes of weathering, erosion, transport, and deposition change Earth’s surface.
  • ES1.K4Earth’s crust is composed of rocks and minerals.
  • ES1.K5Weathering, which can be physical, chemical, or biological, breaks down rocks and minerals into sediments.
  • ES1.K6Erosion removes and transports weathered sediments and debris, creating features
    • on Earth’s surface, e.g., hoodoos
    • beneath Earth’s surface, e.g., caves
  • ES1.K7Erosion is influenced by many factors, including
    • weather and climate
    • hydrological processes
    • features of the land (topography)
    • vegetation cover
    • human activities
  • ES1.K8Mass wasting is a type of erosion in which rocks, soil, or debris move downhill because of gravity, e.g., landslides and rockfalls.
  • ES1.K9Mass wasting can be triggered by many factors, including
    • water
    • earthquakes
    • human activity
  • ES1.K10Deposition occurs when sediments are transported by wind, water, or ice and are dropped in a new location, resulting in landforms such as deltas and dunes.

Skills and procedures

  • ES1.S1Describe local areas that have experienced weathering and erosion.
  • ES1.S2Explain how geological processes change Earth’s surface.
  • ES1.S3Investigate the relationship among various geological processes.
  • ES1.S4Discuss the impact of mass-wasting events that have occurred in Alberta.
  • ES1.S5Suggest solutions to minimize the impact of potential mass-wasting events.
ES2Rc7

Rock Cycle Lab

Transformation of rock through the rock cycle results from geological processes.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (7 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students examine how geological processes continually reshape Earth’s surface.

Knowledge

  • ES2.K1Rocks are formed, broken down, and reformed through specific processes on or below Earth’s surface, which occur at different rates and are referred to as the rock cycle.
  • ES2.K2Rocks are classified as sedimentary, igneous, or metamorphic according to how they were formed and their characteristics, including
    • texture
    • layering
    • mineral composition
  • ES2.K3Sedimentary rock is formed on or just below Earth’s surface through compaction and cementation of sediments.
  • ES2.K4Igneous rock is formed on or below Earth’s surface through cooling and crystallization of molten rock, including magma or lava.
  • ES2.K5Metamorphic rock is formed below Earth’s surface through extreme heat and pressure acting on pre-existing rocks.
  • ES2.K6Common minerals found in rock include quartz, feldspar, mica, and calcite.
  • ES2.K7Minerals can be identified by properties such as colour, lustre, streak, transparency, hardness, and magnetism.

Skills and procedures

  • ES2.S1Explain the rock cycle.
  • ES2.S2Analyze characteristics of local rocks.
  • ES2.S3Classify rocks as sedimentary, igneous, or metamorphic.
  • ES2.S4Investigate how different types of rocks were formed.
  • ES2.S5Identify different minerals from a sample.
ES3Sl7

Soil Health Lab

Geological processes and human activity can affect the nature of soil.

Open the labSlide deck
Curriculum statements (6 knowledge, 4 skills)

Outcome: Students examine how geological processes continually reshape Earth’s surface.

Knowledge

  • ES3.K1Soil is a thin layer of minerals and organic matter, formed over time through weathering, erosion, and decomposition.
  • ES3.K2There are various types of soil, e.g., sand, clay, and loam, which can be described based on physical properties, including
    • composition
    • colour
    • odour
    • pore size

    • parent material

    • food security

Stewardship Capstone

SL2St7

Stewardship Action Lab

Environmental stewardship can address present needs that consider the environment and human well-being.

Open the labSlide deck (opens in a new tab)
Curriculum statements (5 knowledge, 5 skills)

Outcome: Students examine environmental stewardship.

Knowledge

  • SL2.K1Environmental stewardship refers to the shared responsibility of individuals and groups at local, provincial, national, and international levels to protect, restore, and sustainably use the environment and its resources.
  • SL2.K2The protection, restoration, and sustainable use of the environment and its resources can be accomplished through actions such as
    • the creation and maintenance of protected areas to conserve biodiversity
    • waste reduction and circular economy practices
    • responsible water management and conservation
    • the reintroduction of native species
  • SL2.K3A circular economy is a way of designing and using products so that materials are reused, repaired, or recycled instead of being thrown away, helping to reduce waste and protect the environment, e.g., clothing rental and resale programs.
  • SL2.K4Benefits of environmental stewardship include enhanced ecosystem stability and improved human well-being.
  • SL2.K5Environmental stewardship efforts can be motivated by
    • desire to take care of the environment for future generations
    • monetary incentives
    • government regulations

Skills and procedures

  • SL2.S1Explore motivations that inspire environmental stewardship.
  • SL2.S2Research an action that promotes environmental stewardship.
  • SL2.S3Create a plan for engaging in environmental stewardship at home or at school.
  • SL2.S4Investigate how a circular economy contributes to environmental stewardship.
  • SL2.S5Explain the similarities and differences between protecting and caring for something and owning something.
