Part 1 · CS2.K1 · CS2.S1 · CS2.S2

What AI is

How is a program that learns different from one that only follows rules?

Section opener. Ask: what words come to mind when you hear AI? Collect a few on the board, then return to them at the end of this part. Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 1 and 2.

CS2.K1 · Artificial intelligence

AI adapts its actions

AI is the ability of a machine to adapt its actions in response to three things. To adapt means to change to fit new conditions.

Received data

Information it takes in, like sounds, photos or numbers

Prior interactions

What happened the last times it was used

Its environment

The conditions around it right now

Science 7 · CS2 · What AI is

Big idea: AI is not about looking smart. It is about a machine changing what it does based on data and experience. Accuracy flag (CS2.K1): the draft definition is broad. A simple thermostat also responds to data from its environment. The lab teaches AI as a machine that changes its own behaviour from data and experience, beyond fixed rules a person wrote. Definitions of AI vary; many also mention tasks that usually need human intelligence. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 1.

CS2.K1 · Example

Fixed heater vs learning heater

Fixed heater

Always starts at 6:30. If the family sleeps in all week, it still starts at 6:30.

Changes only if a person rewrites its rule.

Learning heater

Starts 30 minutes before the average of the last 5 wake-up times.

Adapts to prior interactions with no new rule.

Example: wake-ups at 8:30 all week → the learning heater soon starts near 8:00.

Science 7 · CS2 · What AI is

Demo the model live: Learn tab, Lesson 1. Enter a week of 8:30 wake-ups and press Next day each time. Ask: which heater was on time more often? Which part of the AI definition does the learning heater show? (Prior interactions.) A start counts as on time if it is 20 to 90 minutes before wake-up. Times are made up for practice.

Science 7 · CS2 · What AI is

Demo: Learn tab, Lesson 2. Switch a few labels on the training messages and watch the learning filter’s word scores change. The rule filter never changes unless a person rewrites its rule. Discussion: Which filter is easier to understand? Which one is easier to fool? Messages are made up for practice.

CS2.S1 · Is it AI?

Three questions to ask

1

Was it trained on data or examples?

2

Does it find patterns to make predictions or decisions?

3

Does its behaviour change with new data or experience?

All three “no”: an ordinary program, even if it seems clever. An automatic door reacts to motion, but always the same way.

Science 7 · CS2 · What AI is

Return to the hook list. Have pairs run each of the six technologies through the three questions and fix their sorting. Answers: calculator, automatic door and crosswalk timer are ordinary programs; music suggestions, voice typing and the learning spam filter use AI. Point out that reacting to a sensor is not the same as learning. Lab link: Lab Bench, Investigation 1 (Is it AI?), nine case files including a wildfire smoke forecast.

Part 2 · CS2.K2 · CS2.S3

Machine learning

What does a machine learn from, and what makes it learn well?

Section opener. Quick poll: who has used an app that names a bird, a plant or a song from a sound or photo? Those apps are trained from data. Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 3 and 4.

CS2.K2 · Machine learning

A type of AI trained from data

Training means showing the program many examples, each with a known answer called a label.

Identify patterns

Chickadee calls cluster together.

Make predictions or decisions

Label a call it has never heard.

Improve over time

More examples, with no new rules written.

Adapt to new data

Retrain when the world changes.

Science 7 · CS2 · Machine learning

The four cards are the four abilities listed in CS2.K2. Accuracy flag (CS2.K2): “without additional programming” is taught as without a programmer writing new rules for the task. People still collect data, retrain and update models. Many apps use a model that stays the same until its makers retrain it, so improvement is not always automatic. Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 3 and 4.

CS2.K2 · Bird call sorter

The 3 nearest calls vote

Each mark is one call with a known label. To label the mystery call, the sorter finds the 3 closest calls and takes the majority.

● Chickadee: higher pitch, shorter
■ Nuthatch: lower pitch, longer

Votes: 1 chickadee, 2 nuthatch → nuthatch

Pitch across, length up. Made-up scores for practice.

Science 7 · CS2 · Machine learning

This shows two abilities from CS2.K2: identifying patterns (each bird’s calls cluster) and making predictions (labelling a call it has never heard). The birds are black-capped chickadee and red-breasted nuthatch, both found around Edmonton, but the pitch and length scores are made up for practice; real bird sound apps use far more features and examples. Ask: what if we add a chickadee call right next to the mystery call? (The vote could flip.) Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 3. Move the mystery call with the sliders and add training calls.

CS2.S3 · Lab Bench investigation

Train it, then test it

FORMULA

accuracy = correct ÷ total × 100%

Test with new calls the sorter never saw in training.

WORKED EXAMPLE

40 test calls, 34 right

34 ÷ 40 × 100% = 85%

Error rate: 6 ÷ 40 × 100% = 15%

Change one variable per trial: training calls, wrong labels, or which calls are included.

Science 7 · CS2 · Machine learning

Students do not need to memorize the formula; it is shown with every problem. Run the investigation together: Lab Bench, Investigation 2 (Train and test a bird call sorter). Suggested trials: 4 calls, then 20, then 60 with clean labels; then 60 with 30% wrong labels; then 20 with “only short calls”. Record in the results table. Each trial uses new random practice data, so results vary a little; the example values are made up. The Practice box below the table gives randomized accuracy and error-rate problems.

CS2.S3 · The role of data

Amount, quality and fit

DataWhat we triedWhat happens
Amount4 calls vs 60 callsAccuracy usually rises, then levels off
Quality30% wrong labelsLearns wrong patterns, accuracy drops
Fit (bias)Only short calls in trainingMost nuthatch calls sorted wrong
New dataNew microphone makes calls longerOld sorter drops; retraining fixes it

Science 7 · CS2 · Machine learning

Bias in data: when training data leaves out or over-represents some cases, predictions can be wrong or unfair for those cases. Connect to real life gently: a face or voice system trained mostly on one group of people may work worse for others. Accuracy flag (CS2.S3): bias in data, wrong labels and data that no longer fits the world are added to explain the role of data; all sorter results are made up for practice. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4 (learning curve and new microphone) and Lab Bench, Investigation 2.

Big idea

A machine learns only what its data shows it.

Too little, wrong or one-sided data teaches the wrong lesson.

Pause here. Ask students to restate the big idea using one of their own trials as evidence. Exit ticket option: “What would you change about the training data to make the sorter fairer?”

Part 3 · CS2.K3 · CS2.K4 · CS2.S4

Narrow AI and general AI

One job done well, or many jobs done like a person?

Section opener. Ask: name an AI tool you used this week. What is the one job it was built for? Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 5 and 6.

CS2.K3 · Narrow AI

Designed for a specific task

Voice recognition

Turns speech into words or commands.

Recommendations

Suggests songs, videos or books from what you liked before.

Chinook

A University of Alberta checkers program that could not be beaten. It could not play chess.

Almost all AI we use is narrow AI. Edmonton is also home to Amii, the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute, where people research machine learning.

Science 7 · CS2 · Narrow and general AI

An algorithm is a set of steps for solving a problem. A recommender learns from prior interactions, which links back to the AI definition. Demo: Learn tab, Lesson 5, book recommender. Like two adventure books and watch the suggestions shift. Accuracy flag (CS2.K3): voice recognition and recommendation algorithms are taught as narrow AI; Chinook and Amii are named as local context, please verify wording before teaching.

CS2.K4 · General AI

Human-like across a wide range of tasks

What it would do

Show human-like intelligence, reasoning and adaptability. Learn a new kind of task the way a person can, and use what it knows in new situations.

Where it is today

A goal researchers talk about. No system is widely accepted as general AI. Experts disagree about whether or when it will be built.

Some chatbots help with many kinds of writing, but they still make mistakes a thinking person would not.

Science 7 · CS2 · Narrow and general AI

Accuracy flag (CS2.K4): general AI is taught as a goal that no system is widely accepted as reaching. Experts disagree about whether or when it will be built. Chatbots that handle many writing tasks are mentioned as harder to classify. This is a fast-changing area, so please re-check before teaching. Sensitive topic note: some students may feel anxious about AI replacing people; keep it calm and factual. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 6. Pick “General AI (an idea)” in the task grid.

Science 7 · CS2 · Narrow and general AI

Have students try Lab Bench, Investigation 3 (Narrow or general: task test). Give the checkers program the task “Turn speech into text” and see it fail. Then try the same tasks with the general AI idea. Ask: what is the biggest difference? (The range of tasks.) Then play Sort It: ordinary program, narrow AI or general AI.

Quick check

Three questions

1

An automatic door opens when a sensor sees motion. Is it AI? Why or why not?

2

A sorter gets 36 of 45 test calls right. What is its accuracy?

3

Is a music recommender narrow AI or general AI? Explain.

Science 7 · CS2 · Review

Answers. 1: No. It reacts to its environment, but always in the same way; it is not trained on data and does not change from experience, so it is an ordinary program. 2: 36 ÷ 45 × 100% = 80%. 3: Narrow AI. It is designed for one specific task (recommending music) and learns from prior interactions; it cannot do other kinds of tasks. Lab link: Quiz tab for ten more questions with explanations.

Debate · Extended response

“General AI will be built in our lifetime.”

Define

Use the meaning of general AI.

Both sides

One reason to agree and one to disagree.

Your view

State it with a reason.

Remember: experts disagree. A strong answer shows why.

Science 7 · CS2 · Review

From the Written Review tab, extended response 3. Run as a four corners debate (strongly agree to strongly disagree), then have students write a paragraph. Model points: some agree because AI tools are improving quickly and already help with many tasks; others disagree because today’s systems still make mistakes a person would not and cannot adapt to any new task like people do. Key-idea checklist: defines general AI, reason to agree, reason to disagree, notes experts disagree, clear personal view with a reason. Keep the tone open; there is no single right answer here.
