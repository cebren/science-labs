Part 1 · M2.K1

What is an element?

One type of atom, and no simpler substance inside.

Section opener. Ask: name something you think is an element. Write a few answers on the board to check later. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 1.

M2.K1 · Element

Elements cannot be broken down

Element

A pure substance made of only one type of atom.

Melt copper and you get liquid copper. It is still copper.

Compound

Two or more types of atom joined together.

Pass electricity through water and it splits into hydrogen and oxygen.

Physical means (melting, filtering, boiling) and chemical means (burning, electricity) cannot break an element into anything simpler.

Science 8 · M2 · What is an element?

Key move: contrast the two cards. Ask why the water result proves water is not an element. Accuracy flag (M2.K1): the lab teaches the statement as written. Nuclear reactions can change one element into another; keep that to a teacher aside if a student asks. “One type of atom” means atoms with the same number of protons; atoms of one element can have different numbers of neutrons. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 1.

Science 8 · M2 · Reading the table

Point out: the table is in order of atomic number, so the whole number goes up by 1 each tile. The atomic mass has decimals because it is an average and atoms of an element can have different numbers of neutrons. Only mercury and bromine are liquids at room temperature; eleven elements are gases. Accuracy flag (M2.K4): bracketed masses such as [98] for technetium are the mass of the longest-lasting isotope of a radioactive element; states for elements 100 and up are not known. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4 (take a tile apart).

M2.K4 · How the order was found

From mass order to atomic number

1869

Dmitri Mendeleev

Ordered the known elements by atomic mass and left gaps for elements not yet found.

1886

Germanium found

It filled one of the gaps, with a mass close to the prediction.

1913

Henry Moseley

Showed that ordering by atomic number works even better. We use that order today.

Science 8 · M2 · Reading the table

Ask: why might mass order cause problems? Tellurium (127.60 u) is heavier than iodine (126.90 u), yet tellurium is 52 and iodine is 53. Atomic number puts them in the right groups. This sets up the debate prompt near the end. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4 note; Lab Bench, Station 4.

Group 18 has a full outer shell of 8. Helium is the exception: its one shell is full with 2.

Groups 3 to 12 are transition metals. They do not follow a simple count.

Science 8 · M2 · Reading the table

The outer shell is the valence shell, and its electrons are valence electrons. Elements in one group react in similar ways because their valence electrons match. Shortcut for groups 13 to 18: subtract 10. Accuracy flag (M2.K5): the lab teaches the group rule for groups 1, 2 and 13 to 18 only, names helium as the exception, and explains that transition metals and the two lower rows do not follow a simple count. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 5 (first 20 elements with Bohr diagrams).

Lab Bench · Station 4

Predict a missing element

Worked example: germanium (32)

Left: gallium, 69.72 u. Right: arsenic, 74.92 u.

estimate = (left + right) ÷ 2

= (69.72 + 74.92) ÷ 2 = 72.32 u

Real germanium: 72.63 u. Very close.

Quick check

Three questions

1

An atom has 8 protons. Which element is it?

2

Magnesium is in period 3, group 2. How many shells and valence electrons does it have?

3

Hydrogen is in group 1. Is it a metal or a non-metal?

Science 8 · M2 · Practice

Answers: 1. Oxygen (atomic number 8). 2. 3 shells and 2 valence electrons, arrangement 2, 8, 2. 3. A non-metal gas, even though it sits in group 1. Use mini whiteboards. For a longer check, students can run a Quiz round (10 questions from a bank of 24). Lab link: Quiz tab.

Debate · Written Review extended 4

Agree or disagree?

“Atomic mass and atomic number are the same thing, so the table could be ordered either way.”

Use evidence from the periodic table and its history. Hint: compare tellurium and iodine.

Science 8 · M2 · Practice

Model answer: Disagree. Atomic number is the number of protons, a whole number that goes up by 1 each tile. Atomic mass is the average mass of the atoms, so it has decimals and includes neutrons. Tellurium (127.60 u) is heavier than iodine (126.90 u), but tellurium is 52 and iodine is 53. Ordered by mass, they would land in the wrong groups. Moseley showed in 1913 that atomic number gives the right order. Run as a four corners vote, then have students write the response. Lab link: Written Review tab, extended response 4.
