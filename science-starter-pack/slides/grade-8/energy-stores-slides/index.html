Science 8 · Energy: Stores, Static and Waves · EN1

Energy stores and transfers

Energy can be transferred between energy stores.

Created by Dr. Lance Pedersen · Use with the Energy Stores Lab

Welcome students and introduce the unit Energy: Stores, Static and Waves. This first understanding sets up the language of stores and pathways used in the static electricity (EN2) and waves (EN3) labs. The eight coloured bars stand for the eight energy stores; each store keeps the same colour in the lab. Ask: where have you heard the word “energy” today? Collect a few uses (energy drinks, “I have no energy”, energy bills) and say we will learn the science meaning. Predict

Where did the toboggan’s energy go?

A rider sits on a toboggan at the top of a 10 m hill in the river valley. They slide down, glide across the flat and finally stop.

A

It was used up by the ride.

B

It is still there, but somewhere else.

C

It went back into the hill.

Science 8 · EN1 · Opening

Give students one minute to vote and write one sentence explaining their choice. Do not reveal the answer yet. The best answer is B: the energy is still there, spread into the thermal store of the snow, runners and air. We return to this on the toboggan slide in the next section. Listen for the misconception that energy is “used up”; the debate slide near the end revisits it with a phone battery. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 1 (the toboggan model) once the reveal comes.

Part 1 · EN1.K1 · EN1.K3

Energy stores

A model for keeping track of how much energy a system has, and where it is.

Section opener. In this part students learn that an energy store is a model (K1) and meet the eight types of store (K3), with Alberta examples for each (S1, S2). Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 1 and 2.

EN1.K1 · A way of keeping track

A store is a model, not a box

What a store is

A label for where a system’s energy is and how much is there. We work out each amount from things we can measure: speed, height, stretch or temperature.

Like bank accounts

Money moves between your chequing and savings accounts, but no coins sit inside. The total only changes when money comes in or goes out.

Add up every store to get the system’s total energy.

Science 8 · EN1 · Energy stores

Key move: energy is a quantity in joules, not a substance (review of Science 7 EN1). A store is a bookkeeping model. Ask: if you cut a moving puck in half, would you find kinetic energy inside? No. We calculate it from mass and speed. Accuracy flag (EN1.K1): the draft says a store shows how much energy is “available” in a system. We teach that stores track how much energy a system has and where it is; not all of it is usable, because energy spread into the thermal store of the surroundings is still there but hard to use again. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 1.

Reveal the hook answer: B. Walk down the table one row at a time. Ask: which store grows as the gravitational store shrinks? (kinetic). Why does the thermal column keep rising? (friction with snow and air resistance warm the runners, snow and air). Point at the Total column: always 5880 J. This is conservation of energy. Where does 5880 J come from? 60 kg × 9.8 N/kg × 10 m. EN1.K3 · EN1.S1

Eight types of energy store

Thermal

Temperature and amount of material

Kinetic

Mass and speed

Elastic

Stretched, squashed, bent or twisted

Gravitational

Mass and height above the ground

Chemical

Fuels, food and batteries that can react

Magnetic

Magnets held apart or pushed together

Electrostatic

Charges held apart or pushed together

Nuclear

The nuclei (centres) of atoms

Each store is linked to something you can observe or measure.

Science 8 · EN1 · Energy stores

Introduce each store with what it depends on. Teaching points: kinetic grows fastest with speed (double the speed, four times the energy). Gravitational strictly belongs to the object and Earth together. Accuracy note (EN1.K3): the chemical store belongs to the fuel or food together with oxygen where the reaction needs it. Magnetic and electrostatic stores are biggest when attracting things are held apart or repelling things are pushed together. The nuclear store is named only (fusion in the Sun, fission of uranium). Memory aid: have pairs invent a sentence using the first letters. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 2 (store gallery). Sort It: Which store fills? deck.

Watch your words

Light, sound and electricity are not stores.

They are ways energy travels from one store to another. Next, we call them pathways.

Accent statement slide. Many students (and many older textbooks) say “light energy”, “sound energy” or “electrical energy” as if they were stores. In this model they are pathways: radiation carries light and sound, and the electrical pathway carries energy by a current. Ask: can you keep light in a jar for later? No. It travels until something absorbs it, and then a store (usually thermal) fills. Lab link: Sort It tab, Store or pathway? deck.

Part 2 · EN1.K4 · EN1.S3

Pathways

Energy moves from one store to another along four pathways.

Section opener. In this part students learn the four pathways named in the draft (heating, radiation, mechanical work, electrical) and practise describing a transfer in three parts: the store that empties, the pathway and the store that fills. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 3.

EN1.K4

Four pathways

Mechanical work

A force moves an object: a push, a pull or a twist.

Electrical

An electric current does work as it flows through a circuit.

Heating

Hotter to colder, by touch (conduction) or moving fluids (convection).

Radiation

Waves such as light and infrared. Sound is often counted here too.

Science 8 · EN1 · Pathways

Give one quick example of each: a hockey stick pushing a puck (mechanical work), a current from a battery to a bulb (electrical), a hot pan warming cold water (heating), sunlight warming your face (radiation). Accuracy flag (EN1.K4): Science 7 EN2 taught radiation as one of three heating processes. In this model, radiation is listed as its own pathway and heating means conduction and convection. Tell students both views describe the same physics. Sound is often grouped with radiation as a wave pathway in this model; waves are studied fully in EN3. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 3; Sort It, Which pathway? deck.

Science 8 · EN1 · Pathways

Model the sentence frame with the flashlight: “Energy is transferred from the chemical store of the battery, by an electrical pathway, to the thermal store of the bulb.” Then continue the chain: radiation (light) carries energy from the bulb to the thermal store of the walls and air. Part 3 · EN1.K2

Store changes and work

Measure how much a store changed, and you know how much work was done.

Section opener. This part links a change in an energy store to the work done (K2), using W = F × d from Science 7 and the gravitational store formula as context only. It ends with the main Lab Bench investigation, the ramp run. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4; Lab Bench, Investigations 2 and 3.

EN1.K2 · Work is energy transferred

Work ends up in a store

W = F × d

work (J) = force (N) × distance (m)

ΔE = m × g × h

increase in the gravitational store, with g ≈ 9.8 N/kg

When a hoist lifts a load at a steady speed, the work done on the load equals the increase in its gravitational store.

Science 8 · EN1 · Store changes and work

Review W = F × d from Science 7 (F2). The force to lift a load at steady speed equals its weight, mass × 9.8 N/kg. So W = weight × height, which is the same as the increase in the gravitational store. Students do not memorize these formulas; they show how the numbers fit together. Accuracy flag (EN1.K2): the draft says a change in stores “can indicate the amount of work done”. We teach this for transfers by a force. Heating and radiation can change stores without any work, which is the next slide. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4.

EN1.K2 · Worked example

Hoist a 25 kg hay bale up 4 m

Step 1

Force = weight = 25 kg × 9.8 N/kg = 245 N

Step 2

W = F × d = 245 N × 4 m = 980 J

Step 3

The bale’s gravitational store increases by 980 J

Same number twice: measuring one tells you the other.

Science 8 · EN1 · Store changes and work

Work through the steps with the class, then try a second case together: a 30 kg canoe lifted 2 m (294 N, 588 J). Ask: where did the energy for the hoist come from? If a person cranks it, the chemical store of food and oxygen in their muscles. Note that the person’s chemical store drops by more than 980 J, because muscles also warm up. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4 (choose the load and drag the height slider); Lab Bench, Investigation 2 for randomized practice.

EN1.K2 · Be precise

Stores can change without work

By work

A hoist lifts a hay bale. A force moves it, so the store change equals the work done.

By heating

A pot of water warms on a stove. Its thermal store grows, but no force moved the water.

Use a store change to find work only when the energy was transferred by a force.

Science 8 · EN1 · Store changes and work

This slide carries the EN1.K2 accuracy flag. The draft says a change in energy stores “can indicate the amount of work done”. That holds when energy moves by mechanical work (or electrical work). Heating and radiation also change stores, and then the change tells you how much energy was transferred, but not work. Ask: sunlight warms a car seat. Did anything do work on the seat? (No. Radiation.) Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 4, the note under the formulas; Quiz includes the stove question.

Lab Bench · Investigation 3 · Ramp run

Where does the gravitational store go?

A 0.50 kg cart rolls down a wooden ramp from 0.60 m. A sensor reads 3.20 m/s at the bottom.

Lost from gravitational

2.94 J

0.50 × 9.8 × 0.60

Gained in kinetic

2.56 J

½ × 0.50 × 3.20²

To thermal by friction

0.38 J

2.94 − 2.56

Made-up practice data, like the simulated trials in the lab.

Science 8 · EN1 · Store changes and work

Main Lab Bench investigation. Walk through the three numbers. About 87% of the gravitational store reached the kinetic store; the rest went by friction to the thermal store of the cart, ramp and air. Ask: what would change on carpet? On a smooth track? (More or less goes to thermal.) Make it a fair test: keep the cart and the height the same and change only the surface. Students run trials themselves and record the table. The kinetic formula is shown for context only. Part 4 · EN1.S3 · EN1.K4

Follow the energy

Real events link several transfers into a chain, and the total never changes.

Section opener. Students trace a full Alberta energy chain, see that energy spreads into the thermal store of the surroundings at every step, and then review with a quick check and a debate prompt. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 5 (step through a chain).

EN1.S3 · Bow River hydro to a Calgary kettle

An energy chain

Gravitational store of water held behind the dam

Mechanical work: falling water pushes the turbine blades

Kinetic store of the spinning turbine

Electrical: the generator drives a current through power lines

Thermal store of water in a kettle

Along the way, friction in the turbine, warm power lines and the kettle body send some energy to the thermal store of the surroundings.

Science 8 · EN1 · Follow the energy

Read the chain from top to bottom, alternating store and pathway. Shaded dashed boxes are pathways; white boxes are stores. Ask students to say each step with the sentence frame. Then have pairs build the prairie wind chain (kinetic store of wind, mechanical work, kinetic store of the rotor, electrical, kinetic store of an Edmonton LRT train). The Bow River dams and wind farms are general contexts, not specific facilities. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 5 (five chains, including Sun to a bike ride and archery).

EN1.K1 · EN1.K4 · Conservation

Spread out, never destroyed

Start of a chain

100%

All in one useful store, like water held behind a dam.

End of a chain

100%

Same total, mostly in the thermal store of the surroundings.

Spread-out thermal energy is hard to use again. That is why no machine passes on all its energy as useful energy.

Science 8 · EN1 · Follow the energy

Link back to the toboggan and the ramp: energy always ends up spreading into thermal stores. This also explains the EN1.K1 flag. A store tracks how much energy a system has, but not all of it is “available” for use; spread-out thermal energy is still there but hard to use. Efficiency as a percentage is not taught in this understanding. Ask: why do we still say people “use up” energy at home? (We use up the useful, concentrated form; the energy itself is conserved.) Quick check

Three questions

1

A drawn bow fires an arrow. Which store empties, which pathway acts, and which store fills?

2

A hoist lifts a 20 kg box 3 m at steady speed. How much does its gravitational store increase?

3

Is “light energy” a store or a pathway? Explain in one sentence.

Science 8 · EN1 · Review

Answers. 1: from the elastic store of the bow, by mechanical work, to the kinetic store of the arrow. 2: weight = 20 × 9.8 = 196 N; W = 196 N × 3 m = 588 J, so the gravitational store increases by 588 J. 3: a pathway. Light is radiation, which carries energy between stores until something absorbs it. Use mini whiteboards or exit slips. Lab link: Quiz tab for a full 10-question round drawn from a bank of 24.

Extended response · Agree or disagree

“When a phone battery goes flat, its energy is used up.”

Your answer should use

the store that empties · the pathways the energy takes · the stores it ends up in · conservation of energy

Science 8 · EN1 · Review

Run as a four-corners debate (strongly agree to strongly disagree), then have students write a paragraph. Model answer: Disagree. Energy is never used up or destroyed. The chemical store of the battery empties, but the energy is transferred by an electrical pathway to the phone’s parts. The screen sends energy out by radiation as light, and the electronics warm up. In the end, the energy is in the thermal store of the phone, your hand and the air. What is used up is the useful, concentrated energy in the battery. Key ideas checklist: clear position; chemical store empties; electrical and radiation pathways; ends in thermal stores of the surroundings; energy conserved but spread out.
