Science 8 · Life Science: Cells and Organ Systems · LS3

Organ systems and health

The interdependent functions of organ systems influence the overall health of an individual.

Created by Dr. Lance Pedersen · Use with the Body Systems Lab

Welcome students. Today’s question: how does your body stay healthy when it is built from trillions of cells that each do one small job? Specialized cells · LS3.K2

Every cell shape has a job

Nerve cell

Long and thin with many branches, so it carries signals far and connects with many cells.

Red blood cell

A flexible disc with no nucleus, so it holds more hemoglobin to carry oxygen and bends through capillaries.

Muscle cell

A long fibre with proteins that slide past each other, so it can shorten with force and pull on bones.

Ciliated airway cell

Hundreds of tiny hairs on top beat together to sweep mucus and dust away from the lungs.

A specialized cell does one job very well, but it cannot do the jobs of other cells.

Science 8 · LS3 · Built in levels

The draft names nerve cells and red blood cells; the other two are extra examples. Ask: what is the link between shape and job in each card? Connect to LS1: mature red blood cells have no nucleus. The lab gallery also shows white blood cells (change shape to wrap around germs) and fat cells (store energy). Drawings in the lab are not to scale. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 2 (specialized cell gallery).

Stem cells · LS3.K3

Stem cells have not chosen a job yet

Embryonic stem cell

Almost any cell

Found in an embryo a few days old. This is how one fertilized egg builds a whole body.

Adult stem cell, like bone marrow

A few related cells

Blood stem cells make only red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.

When a stem cell divides, one daughter cell usually stays a stem cell and the other becomes specialized. This is called differentiation.

1961: James Till and Ernest McCulloch in Toronto showed that blood stem cells exist. Their work helped make bone marrow transplants possible.

Science 8 · LS3 · Built in levels

Accuracy flag (LS3.K3): the draft says stem cells “can specialize into any type of cell.” This is taught more precisely: embryonic stem cells can become almost any cell type; adult stem cells, such as blood stem cells in bone marrow, make only a few related types. Lance: please review the Till and McCulloch (Toronto, 1961) context line. Embryonic stem cell ethics are not discussed in the lab; if students raise it, acknowledge it as a real debate and keep the focus on the science. Discussion prompt: why does the body need stem cells as an adult? (Replace blood cells and skin cells that wear out.) Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 3 (choose each stem cell type, then press Divide).

Interdependent

No organ system works alone.

Each system needs the others to do its job. That is what interdependent means.

Pause here. Ask students to restate “interdependent” in their own words, then give one example from the hook activity (for example, muscles need the circulatory system to bring oxygen). Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 5.

Ask: which system makes these changes happen? (The nervous system senses carbon dioxide and speeds up breathing and the heart.) Why must both speed up? Lab Bench · Investigation 5

How much blood does the heart pump?

B = h × v

B: blood pumped per minute (mL/min)
h: heart rate (beats/min)
v: volume per beat (mL)

You do not need to memorize this.

Worked example

At rest, a student’s heart beats 70 times a minute and pumps 70 mL each beat.

B = 70 × 70 mL
B = 4900 mL/min, about 4.9 L/min

That is close to all the blood in an adult body, every minute.

Your turn: during a jog, h = 120 beats/min and v = 90 mL. Find B in L/min.

Science 8 · LS3 · Seven systems

Answer: B = 120 × 90 mL = 10 800 mL/min = 10.8 L/min. Values are made up for practice. Point out that the formula shows interdependence: when muscles need more oxygen, both h and v go up. The lab also has A = r × t for air breathed per minute (breaths per minute × litres per breath). Lab link: Lab Bench, Investigation 5 (randomized problems with working shown).

Part 3 · LS3.S4

When a system is not working

One problem, many effects. Learning to predict the ripple.

Section opener. Sensitivity note: the next slides describe health conditions in general terms, not as medical advice. Some students may live with asthma, anemia or other conditions, so invite respectful talk and avoid asking anyone to share their own health. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 6.

Example · Asthma attack

One problem ripples through the body

Affected first: respiratory

Airways tighten, swell and fill with mucus, so less air moves in and out.

Then: circulatory

Less oxygen enters the blood, so the heart beats faster.

Then: muscular

Muscles get less oxygen, tire quickly and the person must stop.

Then: nervous

The brain senses carbon dioxide rising and speeds up breathing.

Science 8 · LS3 · When a system is not working

This is general science, not medical advice. Point out that the body tries to make up for the problem (faster heart, faster breathing). The lab ripple predictor has seven more cases: anemia, kidney disease, broken leg bone, stomach flu, concussion, smoking damage and torn hamstring. Sensitivity: some students may live with asthma; avoid singling anyone out. Accuracy flag (LS3.S4): conditions are described in general terms only. Lab link: Learn tab, Lesson 6 (ripple predictor).

Your turn · LS3.S4

Predict the ripple: anemia

Affected first: circulatory

The blood has fewer healthy red blood cells or too little hemoglobin, so it carries less oxygen.

Question 1

What job does this system normally do?

Question 2

Which other systems need that job done?

Question 3

What will those systems do differently?

Science 8 · LS3 · When a system is not working

Give pairs 3 minutes to predict, then reveal. Model answer from the lab: respiratory (breathing speeds up, short of breath on stairs); muscular (tired and weak); nervous (dizziness, trouble concentrating); skeletal (bone marrow works to make more red blood cells, but needs iron). Low iron in the diet is one common cause. General science only, not medical advice. Lab link: Lab Bench, Investigation 4 (predict the ripple, eight random cases with tick boxes and model answers).

Part 4 · LS3.K5 · LS3.S5

Looking inside the body

How X-rays, blood tests and other procedures check on organ systems.

Section opener. A diagnostic medical procedure is a test that helps health workers find out how well an organ system is working, or find a disease or injury. Lab link: Learn tab, Lessons 7 and 8.

X-rays · LS3.K5

Why bones show up white

Drawing: radius (top, with a fracture) and ulna (bottom)

Soft tissue lets most X-rays pass through, so it looks dark grey.

Dense bone absorbs more X-rays, so it looks white.

A break lets X-rays through the gap, so it shows as a dark line.

X-rays are radiation, so the smallest amount needed is used. A CT scan combines many X-ray images into slices.

Quick check

Three questions

1

Put these in order from smallest to largest: organ, cell, organism, tissue, organ system.

2

Can a blood stem cell in bone marrow become a nerve cell? Explain.

3

A person’s kidneys stop filtering well. Predict one effect on another organ system.

Science 8 · LS3 · Review

Answers. 1: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism. 2: No. Adult stem cells like blood stem cells make only a few related types (red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets); embryonic stem cells can become almost any type. 3: Accept any reasoned prediction, for example circulatory (extra water raises blood pressure, swelling in ankles), nervous (wastes in the blood cause tiredness, trouble concentrating) or skeletal (mineral balance is affected, bones can weaken over time). Respond to a classmate · LS3.S3

“Stem cells can turn into any cell, so we could fix any injury with them.”

Do you agree, disagree, or partly agree? Use what you know about stem cells and specialized cells.

Include

What “non-specialized” means and what differentiation is.

Compare

Embryonic stem cells and adult stem cells.

Give an example

A real use of stem cells, and a limit.

Science 8 · LS3 · Review

Use as a four corners debate (agree, partly agree, partly disagree, disagree), then a written response. Strong answers partly agree: stem cells are non-specialized and can differentiate, which is why they matter for growth and repair. Not every stem cell can become any cell: embryonic stem cells can become almost any type, adult stem cells (like blood stem cells) only a few related types. Bone marrow transplants already use blood stem cells to rebuild blood; scientists are still researching how to repair other tissues, so stem cells cannot fix any injury yet. This prompt also addresses the LS3.K3 accuracy flag. Lab link: Written Review tab, extended response 4 (model answer and key ideas checklist).
